At the Market

What the money looks like, how prices are written on tags and stalls, how Thai numbers are built, and what to say when you buy.

The money

The currency is the baht, บาท (bàat), written ฿. A baht is 100 satang, สตางค์ (sà-dtaang), but you'll rarely see satang outside supermarket receipts. Each note has its own color and gets longer as the value goes up; coins get bigger. All drawn to scale, with dollar values at $0.03 to the baht. The money primer covers every note and coin in detail.

Most stalls and shops also take a bank-app QR payment called PromptPay. Look for a printed QR code and the words สแกนจ่าย (sà-gaen jàai, scan to pay).

How Thai numbers work

Thai counts like English up to a point, with a few twists. Learn these and you can read or say any price.

  • Twenty is different. 20 is ยี่สิบ yîi-sìp, not “two-ten”. Every 20-something starts with it: 25 is ยี่สิบห้า.
  • A final one is เอ็ด èt. When 1 ends a bigger number it changes: 11 is สิบเอ็ด, 21 is ยี่สิบเอ็ด, 101 is หนึ่งร้อยเอ็ด.
  • Ten is just สิบ. 10 to 19 never say “one”: 15 is สิบห้า.
  • Thai has single words for 10,000 and 100,000. 10,000 is หมื่น mùuen and 100,000 is แสน sǎen, so 50,000 is “five mùuen”, not “fifty thousand”.
  • Numbers are read biggest first, like English: 350 is สามร้อยห้าสิบ, three-hundred five-ten.
