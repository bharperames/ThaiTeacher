Thai Money

The notes and coins you'll carry in Bangkok, what each is worth in dollars, and practice until prices in baht feel natural.

Thinking in dollars

This app uses 1 baht = $0.03, so about 33 baht to the dollar. The quick way to convert in your head: multiply the baht by 3, then move the decimal point two places left.

450 baht → 450 × 3 = 1,350 → $13.50. Going the other way, dollars × 33 gets you close: $10 → about ฿330.

Banknotes

Five notes, each its own color, and each one longer than the last (drawn to scale here). Tap a note to turn it over. The King's portrait is on every front; the backs show two earlier kings each.

Notes from the previous series, with King Bhumibol (Rama IX) on the front, are still legal tender and still turn up. They use the same colors and values.

Coins

Six coins, drawn to scale. Two series circulate side by side: the 2018 coins have the King's royal monogram on the back, and older ones show a temple.

Coins are drawn as plain value faces in the right colors and sizes, not as copies of the real designs.

What things cost

Rough Bangkok prices to calibrate against. They vary a lot by place; tourist areas and malls cost more.

About the pictures

The Bank of Thailand requires permission to reproduce images of its banknotes, and bars any depiction that could be seen as disrespectful to the monarchy. So the notes here are labeled outlines, marked SPECIMEN, with the portraits left as named frames rather than drawn. For photographs of the real notes, see the Bank of Thailand's banknote pages.
